A string alone is nearly silent: it is far too thin to push air. Its vibration goes through
the **bridge** into the **soundboard**, a large, thin spruce panel that moves the air of the
room. The board decides most of a piano's voice: which frequencies come out strong, how long
notes last, how the bass blooms.

## A plate of modes

The model treats the board as an orthotropic plate — spruce is about twice as stiff along its
grain as across — clamped in the rim. Like the strings, it is a set of modes, each a damped
oscillator; the strings drive them through the bridge, and the board's motion in turn loads
the strings, which is where much of a note's decay comes from.

Its size (`a`, `b`), its surface mass `rho_h` and its stiffnesses `dx`, `dy` set where those
modes fall and how easily the strings move the board: a heavier board gives longer but quieter
notes. The board is also slightly **crowned** — arched, and pressed down by the strings — which
stiffens it like a shell and lifts its lowest modes: `crown_freq`. Below them all, the whole
case moves on its legs as a block, a heavily damped **piston** mode that drinks some energy
from the deep bass (`piston_*`).

## Damping and bridges

`loss_factor` is the board's internal damping (about 2 %), growing with frequency above
`loss_corner`: lower corners dull the highs. The bridge — the hardwood beam where the strings
rest — is stiff, so a string's push is spread along it over a part of a bending wavelength
(`bridge_stiffness`, `bridge_line_mass`, `bridge_spread`) instead of hitting the board at a
point, which smooths the high frequencies. Under the treble, the board is thicker and more
ribbed and moves less: `impedance_slope`, with its counterpart toward the bass
(`impedance_slope_bass`), keeps the bass side from booming like a barrel.

Where the two bridges lie (`treble_bridge`, `bass_bridge`, `main_bridge_from`) decides which
modes each key excites — see the dots on the figure above.

## Uprights

An upright's board stands vertically (`vertical`), behind the action, usually a few centimetres
from a wall. The sound of its back face then bounces off that wall and comes back into the room
(`wall_distance`, `wall_reflection`), and its front is closed by the case: the action, the keys
and panels, through which sound only partly escapes (`case_open`, `case_panel_mass` — the
heavier the panels, the darker and more boxed-in the tone). The method and the measurements
are in [docs/upright.md](https://github.com/mgth/unsampled-piano/blob/main/docs/upright.md).

The `bridge.*` group is only used **without** a soundboard: a measured bridge admittance that
stands in for the board when it is switched off, for analysis.
