The microphones do not stop at the direct sound: they also hear the room. The model places the
piano and its microphones in a **shoebox** hall and computes what reaches them in two parts —
the first echoes exactly, the late reverberation statistically.

<RoomView />

## Early reflections

Each wall reflection is the sound of an **image** of the piano mirrored in that wall, arriving
later and weaker; reflections of reflections are images of images. The model computes them up
to `order` (3 by default), each with its own wall filtering: walls absorb more of some
frequencies than others (`absorption_low` around 125 Hz, `absorption_high` around 4 kHz). For
the reflections the piano is a point source at the board's centre; `piano` places the board in
the room, and near a wall its echoes come early and strong.

## Reverberation

After the first echoes, a 16-line **feedback delay network** takes over and builds the dense
tail. Its decay follows Sabine's formula, T60 = 0.161·V / (S·α), with the air absorbing more of
the highs; `tail_gain` scales it (1 is physical: the reverberant energy is set from the room's
critical distance).

## Directivity

A piano does not radiate evenly. A grand with its lid open beams its sound towards the audience
side; an upright against a wall spreads it. Since the room is driven by the sound the
microphones hear, `directivity` (a factor Q) says how much of it the piano sends everywhere
else: the higher Q, the less it fills the room. 0 lets the instrument decide — 3 for a grand
with its lid open, 2 with it closed or removed, 2 for an upright against a wall, 1.5 standing
free.

The *Room* listening presets are these halls; *Dry* turns the room off and leaves the piano
alone with its microphones.
