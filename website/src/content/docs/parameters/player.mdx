The **Player** page is the touch of the instrument: how a MIDI velocity becomes a hammer
speed, what the three pedals do, how quickly an upright repeats a note, and the overall
level. All of these are **live**: the engine applies them while you play, and in the plugin
they are host parameters you can automate.

## From velocity to hammer speed

A MIDI keyboard sends a velocity from 1 to 127; a piano only knows how fast its hammer flies.
The map between the two is exponential — the same law recording pianos use — so that a MIDI
file recorded on a Disklavier keeps its dynamics:

<p class="formula">v<sub>hammer</sub> = vmin · (vmax / vmin)<sup>(velocity − 1) / 126</sup></p>

A faster hammer does not just play louder: it squeezes its felt harder, and felt stiffens the
more it is squeezed, so a fortissimo is also much **brighter**. That is why raising `vmax`
changes the timbre of the loudest notes, not only their level — see the [hammer](/docs/parameters/hammer/).

## The sustain pedal

The right pedal (CC 64) lifts every damper off the strings. It is continuous: between resting
and lifted there is a **half pedal** where the dampers only brush the strings and notes die
slowly — pianists use it all the time. Three parameters shape it.

<PedalCurve />

How quickly a damper actually falls back or lifts, and how hard it grips, is on the
[Dampers](/docs/parameters/dampers/) page.

## The soft pedal and the middle pedal

The left pedal (CC 67) works differently on a grand and on an upright, and the model does
both; presets choose with `una_corda_shift`.

<SoftPedal />

The middle pedal (CC 66) is a **sostenuto** on a grand: it keeps up the dampers of the keys
held when it goes down. Many uprights use it instead to lower a strip of felt between hammers
and strings, for practising quietly: give `mute_felt` a thickness and the middle pedal becomes
that practice mute.

## Repetition

A grand's action has a double escapement: a key can strike again before it has come all the
way up. An upright's cannot, so a note repeated too soon after its release comes out weaker.
`repetition_time` is that recovery time (0 on a grand), `repetition_floor` how strong an
immediate re-strike still is.

`output_gain_db` is the last stage: a gain after the room, smoothed so automation never
clicks. It does not change the tone.
