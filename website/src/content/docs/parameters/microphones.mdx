There is no "stereo width" knob in Unsampled Piano, because there is no stereo trick: the left
channel is what a microphone at one point in space hears, the right channel what a second one
hears. Each microphone sums the sound of every soundboard mode by the **Rayleigh integral** —
the board pushing the air, patch by patch, each patch arriving with its own delay and
distance — plus the reflections off the lid, the frame and the rim, then the room.

Move a microphone and the balance between bass and treble, the attack and the sense of
distance all change, exactly as they would at a real piano. Changing a position rebuilds the
radiation weights, which takes a fraction of a second.

## Set-ups

The *Listening* presets are microphone set-ups you can combine with any instrument: loading one
keeps the instrument, loading an instrument keeps the set-up.

<MicPlan />

Positions are in metres, in the soundboard's own coordinates: `x` along the strings from the
keyboard end, `y` across from the treble side, `z` above the board. With `mics_relative` on, `x`
and `y` are fractions of the board's length and width instead, so a set-up follows the board
when you change instrument — the listening presets use that. `mic_gain_db` levels the set-up:
close microphones hear the piano about 13 dB louder, so the close preset turns itself down.

## The lid

<LidSection />

`lid_angle` = 0 takes the lid out of the model altogether: no reflection, no shadow. That is
the default instrument, and the *audience, lid closed* set-up.
