Each string is a set of **modes** — its partials — each a damped oscillator computed in
complex form, thousands of them per note. The strings of one key are coupled through the
bridge they share, and each vibrates in two directions. Nothing here is a filter on a recorded
tone: what you hear is that system ringing.

<StringAnatomy />

## Scale

The **scale** is the set of string lengths. From the top down, each octave is longer by
`ratio_per_octave` (an ideal string would double; real ones do not, or a grand would be ten
metres long). Below `break_note` the case runs out of room: the bass strings are shortened
toward `len_a0` and made heavier by winding copper around them. With the tension and the pitch,
the length gives each string its mass, μ = T / (2·L·f0)².

## Unisons

Most keys have two or three strings, struck together. They are never exactly in tune with each
other, and should not be: that slight spread is what gives a piano tone its life.

`hit_spread` is the other half of the story: the hammer never strikes the strings of a key
evenly, and the uneven part sets them swinging against each other from the start.

## Two polarisations, two decays

<DoubleDecay />

The `polarization.*` parameters set how weakly the bridge holds the sideways motion
(`h_coupling`), how much energy passes between the two directions (`cross` — clamped so the
bridge stays passive), and how much of the sideways motion is heard.

## Losses

Strings lose energy on their own too — air drag, internal friction — on top of what the bridge
and soundboard take. The intrinsic decay rate of a partial at frequency *f* is

<p class="formula">σ = b1_floor·w(n) + e<sup>b1_slope·n + b1_offset + top(n)</sup> + b2·f²</p>

with the T60 = 6.9 / σ. The `b2` term makes the high partials die sooner, so the sound
darkens as it decays; `b1_floor` adds the losses of the wound bass strings; `top_slope` those
of the top strings, which have no dampers and would otherwise ring on after the keys are
released.

## Nonlinearity

Struck hard, a string stretches a little as it swings, and its tension rises and falls twice
per cycle. That drives the string's **longitudinal** modes with sums and differences of its
partials: the "phantom" partials that give a loud bass its growl. The model computes them up to
`max_note` (E4 by default; above, they are too high to matter), with a level that grows as the
square of the amplitude — they are there at forte, gone at piano.
