A damper is a felt pad resting on the strings near the keyboard end. Pressing a key lifts it;
releasing the key lets it fall back and stop the sound. The sustain pedal lifts all of them at
once. In the model a damper is a **dashpot** on the string: it adds to partial *k* a decay rate
proportional to how much that partial moves under the pad, sin²(kπx/L) averaged over its width.

## Grip

`strength` is how firmly the felt stops the string. It is not "more is better": a damper is
most absorbing when its resistance matches the string's own impedance (√(T·μ), strength 1).
Softer and notes linger after release; much harder and the felt starts to reflect the vibration
back instead of absorbing it.

`contact_exponent` shapes how the damping grows as the felt settles: with a high exponent, a
damper that only brushes the string barely stops it — which makes the half pedal usable and
note endings softer. `node_floor` catches the partials that have a still point right under the
pad and would otherwise escape it.

## Sympathetic resonance

With the dampers lifted, every string is free to answer the ones being played: a C3 struck with
the pedal down sets ringing the strings whose partials coincide with its own — C4, G4, C5, E5…
and C2 or F1 below, whose upper partials meet its fundamental. That is the halo of a pedalled
piano.
