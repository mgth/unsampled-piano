The strings of a grand pull with close to twenty tonnes in all. A **cast-iron frame** (the
plate) takes that load, and a thick laminated **rim** — the curved wooden wall of the case —
holds the soundboard at its edge. Neither is meant to sound, but both vibrate, and you hear
them: a little metallic colour from the frame, some low and middle body from the rim.

<FrameRim />

## The frame

The frame is modelled with the same modal machinery as the board: an isotropic clamped plate
(`frame.a`, `frame.b`, `rho_h`, `stiffness`, `loss_factor`), driven at the agraffes by the
strings' force, and radiating from its own height above the board. Only its metal radiates —
`solidity` is the share of its outline that is not openings — and its underside largely cancels
its top through those openings (`back_gain`). The default puts its first mode near 150 Hz.

## The rim

The rim is a ring following the board's perimeter, bending out of its plane and twisting
(Love's theory for curved beams), driven by the forces the clamped board's edges exert on it.
Its four faces radiate — the inner one half hidden under the board (`inner_gain`). Its size
(`height`, `thickness`, `top`) and its laminated maple (`young`, `shear`, `density`) set its
modes; `k_max` and `f_max` limit how many are computed.

Both are **one way**: the frame and the rim take their motion from the strings and the board
but do not feed it back, so they colour the sound without changing how long the notes last.
`frame.gain` and `rim.gain` scale what they radiate (1 is physical). The derivations are in
[docs/frame_rim.md](https://github.com/mgth/unsampled-piano/blob/main/docs/frame_rim.md).
