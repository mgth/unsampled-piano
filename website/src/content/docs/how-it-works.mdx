Unsampled Piano contains **no recordings**. Every sound it makes is computed while you play,
from a model of the parts of a piano and the way they push on each other. Recordings of real
pianos are used for one thing only: measuring, to set the model's parameters and check them.

## The chain

<SignalChain />

1. **Hammer.** A key's velocity becomes a hammer speed. The hammer's felt is a nonlinear,
   hysteretic spring (Stulov), layered with a hard core; a passive contact solver integrates
   the strike against the strings, sample by sample. → [Hammer](/docs/parameters/hammer/)
2. **Strings.** One to three stiff strings per key, each a bank of modes with its own
   inharmonicity, losses and two directions of vibration, coupled to the other strings of its
   key through the bridge (Weinreich): the double decay and the beating come out of that
   coupling, not from envelopes. Loud bass notes add their phantom partials. →
   [Strings](/docs/parameters/strings/), [Tuning](/docs/parameters/tuning/)
3. **Dampers and pedals.** Dampers are dashpots on the strings; the sustain pedal is continuous;
   with the dampers up, every free string listens to the bridge (sympathetic resonance). →
   [Dampers](/docs/parameters/dampers/), [Player](/docs/parameters/player/)
4. **Soundboard.** A clamped orthotropic plate, modal, driven by the bridges, loading the strings
   in return. The cast-iron frame and the rim radiate too. →
   [Soundboard](/docs/parameters/soundboard/), [Frame & rim](/docs/parameters/frame-rim/)
5. **Radiation.** Two microphones in space hear the board through the Rayleigh integral, with
   its back face, the lid's reflection and shadow. → [Microphones](/docs/parameters/microphones/)
6. **Room.** Image sources for the early reflections, a feedback delay network for the tail. →
   [Room](/docs/parameters/room/)

Mechanical noises — keybed, dampers, pedal — are impulses into the same strings and board.
→ [Noises](/docs/parameters/noises/)

## A modal engine

Strings, board, frame and rim are all written the same way: as **modes**, damped oscillators
each computed as a complex one-pole recursion, *z* ← *p*·*z* + *b*·*x*. The complex form keeps
the frequencies exact in single precision down to A0, and it vectorises: modes are packed in
chunks that the CPU advances together with AVX2 or AVX-512. One core of a recent desktop CPU
runs several hundred thousand modes in real time; a whole piano with every string free is a few
percent of it. Results are **bit-identical** whatever the instruction set, which makes offline
renders reproducible.

What is costly is not playing but **building**: computing the string modes, the board's modes,
their coupling and the radiation weights for two microphones. Changing a "rebuild" parameter
does that in the background — about a tenth of a second — and swaps the new instrument in
without cutting held notes.

## Calibration

The default instrument is fitted, step by step, on measurements:

- **Salamander Grand** — recordings of a Yamaha C5, analysed note by note: partial frequencies
  (inharmonicity, stretch), decay rates of every partial (ESPRIT), prompt sound and aftersound.
- **Iowa** — a second set of real piano recordings, for the spread between instruments.
- **A commercial modelled piano**, recorded through its ten acoustic models, as a reference for
  timbre at every dynamic, for pedal behaviour and for the range of instruments.

Each step is a document in the repository — the hypothesis, the measurement, what changed and
by how much: [the list is in the README](https://github.com/mgth/unsampled-piano#documentation).

## Honestly: what is not right yet

The model sits within the spread of the references on most measures, not everywhere. Known gaps, as of October 2026:

- the top octave (C7–C8) is too quiet at *pp*, and its held notes die a bit too fast;
- in the treble (C5–B6) at *mf*, the end of a held note is 6–10 dB short at 3 seconds;
- in the middle register, partials 8–12 are 5–8 dB low during the sustain;
- the bass ends of note are 2–3 dB short at 3 seconds.

Structural work still ahead: a felt whose stiffness grows gradually instead of two layers, a
realistic soundboard (ribs, varying thickness, finite-element modes), and the duplex scale. If
you know pianos — or physics — and hear something wrong, an
[issue](https://github.com/mgth/unsampled-piano/issues) is very welcome.
