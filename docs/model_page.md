# The Model page

The interface's **Model** page draws the instrument as the model builds it, to scale, in three modes:

- **Static**: every part where the parameters put it (soundboard, bridges, strings from their agraffe to their bridge point, hammers at the strike point, dampers, frame, rim, lid, microphones), with the model in numbers beside it. Pointing at a parameter elsewhere lights its part; clicking a part's name opens its page.
- **Live**: the same instrument with the engine's state laid on it as it plays: keys and pedals down, hammers on the strings, dampers lifted, each key's string energy (the strings' motion is slowed down and enlarged to be seen, it is not their real motion) and the soundboard's velocity from its 96 lowest modes, against the loudest of the last seconds.
- **Editor**: the plan with handles on the geometry (board length and width, the outline's vertices, the main and bass bridge points, the agraffes, the frame's end, the microphones) and the geometric parameters beside it. Each move rebuilds the instrument once the edits pause, as any other rebuild parameter.

The board and the rim follow the board's outline (`soundboard.outline`, docs/outline.md), the full rectangle when none is set; the editor's **Board outline** buttons set a grand's or go back to the rectangle, and a handle on each vertex moves it. The lid has the case's shape, open or shut, as the radiation has it (docs/lid.md). On a grand the case goes on above the rim (`rim.top`) up to the lid's hinge (`soundboard.lid_hinge_height`), the box the shut lid closes, so that the lid rests on it; only the rim below is in the structure's model. The iron frame is drawn as a grand's plate is cast, though the model's frame is a plate of some mass and stiffness at the frame's height with no shape of its own: low, from just in front of each bridge's hitch pins out to the walls, the plate they stand on, cut where it is broad by round holes with raised rims (up to 9 cm across) in a row along the curve; across the front, between the hammers and the dampers, a bar standing on edge; from it, struts on edge too (1.1 cm thick, 4.5 cm deep all along, a flange along their tops), along the strings: seen from the keyboard, one either side of the bass strings, at their slant; one on the left of the tenor's lowest strings, under the bass strings, at the tenor's slant; two among the treble's, in the gaps between unisons, a third and two thirds of the way from where they are clear of the bass strings to the top; one past the last string on the right; crossing where they will; each flares where it leaves the bar, runs level over the dampers, slopes gently down to just over the strings at its bridge and, just past it, comes down onto the plate, with no frame along the walls. Their undersides clear the dampers' heads lifted (8.9 cm) and, past them, the strings. Pointing at a frame parameter lights it. What the model has no geometry for is drawn plainly: the strings start at their key's place across the keyboard, which crosses the bass strings over the tenor as on an overstrung piano; legs, keyboard and pedals only situate the instrument.

A grand's keys and action are drawn key by key as most are made, a double escapement, to scale; the model has none of it but the hammers' line, their blow, the dampers' place on the strings and the engine's state, so the rest only situates them. Each key, cranked from the keyboard's spacing to the strings' (the action's pitch is even, C8's and the main bridge's lowest key's hammers where their strings cross the keys' line; the bass's closer together when a board too narrow for them would bring A0's agraffe within 2.5 cm of the case's wall, which the engine never sees: it drives the frame in line with the bass strings' bridge points), pivots on the key frame's balance rail; its capstan lifts the wippen, which carries the jack and the repetition lever; the jack, its toe under the let-off button, pushes the knuckle under the hammer's shank; the shank pivots on its flange rail, the hammer resting on the rest rail; on the key's tail the backcheck catches the hammer as it falls, and behind it the key's end lifts the damper's underlever, hinged further back on the damper rail, by the cloth under its wire block; the wire goes straight up through the gap between two unisons and across into the side of the damper head, centred over it. The head rests where the engine damps the string: the share of its speaking length `damper.behind_strike` gives (6 cm behind the strike point, on the engine's length), laid on the string as drawn, which is longer (it runs from the hammers' line to the bridge): the wires stand 5.8–7 cm behind the hammers', behind the backchecks. The head, as long as the engine's pad (`damper.width`), is a gendarme's hat in profile: its felt in two pads either side of a slit, a red cloth over them, the wood low at its ends and rising to a crown over the hole the wire goes into. The felt is cut to its strings: a single string sits in a groove, a bichord's wedge goes down between its two strings, a trichord's double wedge between its three, the middle one in the notch between the points, and from G4 up the thin treble strings take a flat felt. Brackets between keys hold the rails. A grand's fallboard bears the name, as makers inlay theirs: the logo (`assets/icon.svg`) and "Unsampled" in gold leaf a quarter of a millimetre proud of the lacquer, the word's capitals 2 cm high; the **Fallboard** box shuts it over the keys (a board from high on the name board down to just in front of them, a lip onto the key slip), the name then on it. Above the fallboard the front goes on up to the case's top, under the lid, and a cover runs from there back over the pin block to the hammers' line, hiding the tuning pins as a real grand's front does; the **Front see-through** slider over the view makes both see-through (0 % solid, 100 % left out). The lid, see-through in the drawing, is solid lacquer in the realistic look, which draws this front as see-through as asked. The word's outlines come from Noto Serif Display Medium (SIL Open Font License), flattened and cut into triangles once by `tools/lettering.py` into `model/scene/lettering.rs`, so that the interface needs neither the font nor a font engine. A white key's wood is covered by its keytop, 2 mm thick, its front edge 1.5 mm past the wood and rounded, over a front plate 1 mm thick that covers the key's front (the sizes replacement keytops are sold in, and the fitters' 1.5 mm overhang gauge); a black key's cap tapers by 1.2 mm a side up to its top, whose long edges are rounded (2 mm), its front sloping back 11 mm over a rounded edge. A key's lead weights are more in the bass, its hammer larger by its mass. Live, a key down turns about its balance rail, lifting its wippen; its hammer is on the strings, checked or at rest as the engine says, its jack escaped; its damper rises with the key or the pedal, and the soft pedal shifts the keys and the action towards the treble. The hammers' line stands 4.5 cm in front of the board's edge, room for a real hammer (its head leaning back at rest and forward on the strings) to swing past it; their crowns rest 5 cm under the treble's strings, 7 under the bass's (higher, overstrung), the bass's heads taller to reach them. The keyboard keeps cheek blocks at least 3 cm wide inside the case's walls, its arms in line with the rim: it moves towards the treble or the bass as far as needed, centred when there is no more room (the default board, 1.30 m across for a 1.23 m keyboard), and its keys crank to their hammers behind it. The **Case** box leaves the case (rim, arms, fallboard, belly rail) out to show the action; with the case, only what shows through it is drawn (keys, hammers, dampers), the rest being hidden.

An upright is drawn the same way, its board standing with its bottom edge 25 cm above the floor (the room's `VERTICAL_FOOT`), its keys' tops at 72 cm. Its action is an upright's as most are made: the hammers' shanks stand on their butts in front of the strings, pivoting on flanges on the main rail, the hammers striking on the hammers' line (the treble's shanks upright on the strings, the bass's leaning towards the pianist to reach their strings, nearer); at rest their crowns lie 4.5 cm from their strings against a rest rail half way up the shanks, one per section, which the soft pedal moves half way to the strings when the model has a half blow; under the butts the wippens pivot under the main rail, their jacks under the butts, their heels on the keys' capstans through stickers as tall as the hammers' line stands above the keys (nearly 40 cm on the upright presets, whose board's top stands 1.30 m up); brackets between keys hold the main rail from the keybed. The dampers are under the hammers, where the engine damps (`damper.behind_strike` below the strike), their heads the grand's, on levers between the shanks and the strings hinged above the butts, a wire from the lever's top into the head; the key or the pedal swings them off the strings towards the pianist. The rim stands behind the board, its back's frame (its height the model's, its front 1.5 cm in front of the board), and the plate's bar and struts lie low over the strings, in front of the bass strings, the hammers and dampers between them. The case is an upright's: two sides from the floor up past the tuning pins, coming forward beside the keys as arms; the top over them; the fallboard (or the name board, open) behind the keys and the upper panel above it in front of the action; the keybed, key slip and cheek blocks; the lower panel under the keybed down to the plinth, the pedals coming out of it through brass plates. **Front see-through** makes the panels and the top see-through; **Case** leaves the case out.

## Views

The plan, an orthographic view of the board's face (from above on a grand, from the front on an upright), is drawn by egui itself and works everywhere. The **3D** view turns around the instrument (drag to turn, right-drag to move, scroll to come closer, double-click to frame it again) and is drawn by the GPU, through egui's own OpenGL painter: an offscreen multisampled target with a depth buffer, copied into the view. It needs OpenGL 3.3 or GLES 3.0; if the context cannot do it, the page says why and shows the plan.

### The realistic look

The **Realistic** box draws the 3D view as a photograph of the instrument in a studio (`model/real.rs`); the drawing is the same scene, only its shading changes. Each part is shaded as what it is made of: the scene tags every vertex with a material (`scene::Material`), most from the colour the part is drawn in, the rest (the board, strings, keys, hammers' felt, pedals) set by the part itself so that a part pointed at or a level keeps its material. Black lacquer under a polished clear coat that waves a little; the board's spruce in panels some 11 cm wide, its growth rings and fibres, under a satin varnish; hard wood for the action, keys' sticks and bridges; the plate's gold bronze paint; steel, copper, brass, lead; felt, matte with a fuzz at grazing angles; leather; polished white keys letting a little light through, satin black ones. The strings are wires as thick as their mass per length makes them (as if all steel; a wound string's plain core beyond its bridge and round its pin), the wound ones' turns ridged along them; drawn at least two pixels wide, a thinner wire is blended by the share of them it covers, its highlight spread as a pixel sees the whole of its round (strings drawn by sample coverage alone came out dotted). Every highlight is kept no sharper than its surface's normal turns over a pixel, so that small curved parts (pins, agraffes) do not sparkle.

The studio has four softboxes: a key light high in front on the treble's side, which casts the shadows (a 2048² shadow map, the penumbra growing with the distance from what casts it, 3.5 cm per metre), a dim fill in front on the bass's side, two strips behind that draw the case's edges; grey walls and a darker ceiling. The polished parts reflect them (the key light's reflection shadowed as its light is); over a grand's board and plate, what looks down sees them lit rather than the floor. The recesses are darkened by an ambient occlusion from the view's depth, at two reaches (3 cm between parts, 30 cm under the case). A floor of dark planks takes the shadows and reflects the instrument (drawn again from the eye's mirror image, fading with the height above the floor), and fades far off into the room. All of it is drawn in floating point, multisampled (8 samples where the GPU has them, as the plain look), then exposed, given a little bloom on the brightest highlights, tone-mapped (ACES) and dithered.

In the live view the board's motion and the strings' levels glow amber over their materials, as far as their colours went from rest towards the highlight. The drawing's strokes are left out; the lid is solid lacquer; the plate is in bronze paint, half metallic under a lacquer. It costs some 2.5 ms more a frame than the plain look on a discrete GPU (5.8 against 3.3 ms at 764 × 781, most of both being the scene's upload); on a context that cannot draw in floating point the page says why and shows the plain look.

For visual checks without a window, `PIANO_SHOT_DIR=<dir> PIANO_SHOTS='<name>:<yaw>,<pitch>,<dist>,<x>,<y>,<z>[,<key down>,<case>,<cut>]' cargo test -p piano-ui --lib shots -- --ignored` draws the scene by software into `<dir>/<name>.ppm` (a negative distance: orthographic, that many metres high; a cut leaves out what lies across below it, a section through the action).

The GPU's drawing, realistic look included, is checked in a window: `PIANO_UI_PAGE='Model realistic hide=names,mics orbit=<yaw>,<pitch>,<dist>,<x>,<y>,<z>' PIANO_UI_SHOT=<file.ppm> cargo run -p piano-ui --features gpu --example demo` saves the window once drawn (`live` or `editor` for the mode, `fallboard` to shut it, `front=<0…1>` for the front's see-through, `hide=` any of names, lid, case, frame, mics).

The 3D view is in `piano-ui`'s `gpu` feature and the host turns it on (`PianoUi::enable_gpu`): the standalone player does when eframe paints with glow. The plugin does not yet: its editor shows the plan.

## The engine's state

`Engine::observe` fills a `piano_core::Observation` (no allocation): per key, down, hammer on the strings, damper engagement and string energy; the pedals; the modulus of the board's lowest modes' states, in the order of `Soundboard::new`. The audio side (`EngineSlot`) publishes it about 60 times a second into atomics shared with the controller, summing the engines still finishing their notes after a rebuild; the interface reads it with `PianoControl::observe` and evaluates the modes' shapes on the board's grid. The interface's mock control plays an arpeggio by itself, so the live view can be worked on without an engine (`cargo run -p piano-ui --features gpu --example demo`, `PIANO_UI_PAGE="Model live"`).
